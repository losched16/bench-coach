## Draft 1 — Skater Step and Stick

| Field | Draft |
|---|---|
| Name | Skater Step and Stick |
| Skill category | Athletic Development |
| Primary skill | lateral landing control |
| Secondary skill | single-leg balance |
| Age range (advisory) | 7-14; easier for younger players |
| Difficulty / progression level | Beginner to Intermediate / 2 |
| Equipment | two cones or tape marks about 2 to 3 feet apart; flat, dry, non-slip surface |
| Space | small; fits the indoor 20×15 ft space |
| Players / coaches | 1 player, 1 supervising adult |
| Time | about 4 minutes |
| Format | individual |

**Description.** The athlete stands on one leg beside a mark, steps sideways to
the other mark, lands on the outside foot with the knee over the toes and the
hips back, and holds the landing quietly for two seconds. Then they step back the
other way. There is no hop: a step, a soft landing and a still hold. This is the
sideways landing a fielder or runner uses to stop and change direction.

**Coaching notes.** Cue: "step, soften, hold". Watch the landing knee: it should
track over the middle of the foot and not cave inward. The landing should be
quiet; a loud slap means the athlete is dropping onto the foot instead of
absorbing it. Keep the chest tall and the trailing foot off the ground. If the
athlete cannot hold for two seconds, shorten the distance rather than adding
speed. Do the whole set on one side before switching so the pattern is clear.

**Safety.** Dry, flat, non-slip surface and grippy shoes. Stop for knee, ankle
or hip pain, or if the knee keeps collapsing inward. No hop until controlled
landings have been observed in two sessions (the program's "skater hop"
unlock). Never add speed and distance together.

**Success markers.**
- Lands on the outside foot with the knee over the toes
- Holds the landing for two seconds without wobbling
- Lands quietly on most reps

**Reps guidance.** 2 sets of 3 each side, about 45 to 60 seconds between sets.

**Regression.** Smaller step (about one foot), hands on hips, or one hand on a
wall for balance.

**Progression.** Larger step, then (only after the unlock) a small hop with the
same quiet, held landing. Later, a coach-called direction.

**Mechanic focus.** landing mechanics, knee tracking, hip hinge, single-leg
balance.

**Common flaws.** knee collapsing inward; loud, stiff landing; reaching with the
trailing foot to catch balance; rushing the hold.

**Tags.** athletic development, landing, balance, lateral, beginner.

## Draft 2 — Half-Circle Cut (Round the Cones)

| Field | Draft |
|---|---|
| Name | Half-Circle Cut — Round the Cones |
| Skill category | Athletic Development |
| Primary skill | cutting and change of direction |
| Secondary skill | deceleration, reacceleration |
| Age range (advisory) | 8-14 |
| Difficulty / progression level | Intermediate / 3 |
| Equipment | three cones in a shallow arc, about 3 yards between cones (about 2 yards indoors) |
| Space | medium outdoors; a shortened arc fits the indoor 20×15 ft space |
| Players / coaches | 1 player, 1 supervising adult (a partner is optional for the reaction version) |
| Time | about 6 minutes |
| Format | individual or small group, taking turns |

**Description.** Three cones are set in a shallow half-circle. The athlete moves
from the first cone to the second and third, planting the outside foot at the end
of each leg and pushing off it to run the next leg, finishing with a short
acceleration out. The movement is a series of controlled plants, not a smooth
curve. It teaches the lower hips, the outside-foot plant and the push-out that
turn a fielder toward a ball or a runner around a base.

**Coaching notes.** Cue: "plant, push, up". First walk it, then jog it, then
add speed. Watch which foot plants: it is the foot on the outside of the turn.
Lower the hips into each plant and bring the chest up on the way out. The common
fault is rounding the whole arc into a lazy curve; set the cones so rounding
them is clearly wrong. Quality over speed: stop the rep when the plants turn
into skids.

**Safety.** Dry, flat surface with good footing and proper shoes; do not cut
hard on wet grass or a slick floor. Start at 70 to 85 percent effort in week one.
Stop for knee or ankle pain, slipping, or loss of form. Indoors, shorten the arc
and keep a clear stopping area past the last cone.

**Success markers.**
- Plants the outside foot on most cones
- Keeps the hips low through the plant and the chest up on the exit
- Exits each plant accelerating instead of drifting

**Reps guidance.** 2 each direction, 45 to 60 seconds between reps.

**Regression.** Walk, then jog the arc, planting deliberately at each cone.

**Progression.** Add speed gradually, then a partner-rolled ball at the last cone
so the athlete reacts, then a catch or throw out of the final plant. Never add
speed and the reaction in the same week.

**Mechanic focus.** plant foot selection, hip lowering, reacceleration, knee
tracking.

**Common flaws.** rounding the arc; planting the inside foot; standing tall
through the plant; skidding instead of pushing.

**Tags.** athletic development, change of direction, cutting, intermediate.
