# Indoor falling start: review and proposal

**Status: change made in the template, pending coach approval.**

## The problem

The source specification gives an indoor substitute for the 10-yard
acceleration ("3 reps of a controlled 2-3-yard takeoff and brake"). It says
nothing about the falling start, which is a 5-yard start. The first version of
the program copied the same substitute for the falling start without checking
whether it was safe.

The indoor space is 20 ft by 15 ft. Five yards is 15 ft, the whole short side,
so a 5-yard start leaves no stopping room. A falling start also begins by
leaning past balance, which on a hard or slick floor with a wall nearby is the
riskiest moment of the drill.

## Options considered

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Keep the 5-yard start indoors | Rejected: no stopping room. |
| Reuse the 2-3-yard takeoff and brake (previous text) | Better, but it still invites a full-lean fall followed by up to 9 ft of run on a 15 ft side. |
| **Two-step lean-and-catch** (proposed) | Keeps the skill (lean, then first steps) at the lowest speed that still teaches it. About 2 yards, along the 20 ft length, 10 ft clear past the stop line. |
| Drop the falling start indoors and add a wall-drive set | Safest, and kept as the fallback when the floor or space is not right. |

## What the app now says indoors

> Lean-and-catch. Lean forward from a standing stance, take two quick low steps
> (about 2 yards) and land balanced on two feet. Use the 20 ft length and keep
> 10 ft clear past the stop line. Grippy footwear, dry non-slip floor. If the
> floor is slick or the space is tight, skip it and add one wall-drive set
> instead.

Same number of reps (4) and rest (45-60 s) as outdoors. The 10-yard acceleration
keeps the source's own indoor text unchanged.

## Why this is the safest practical choice

- the lean is smaller and ends in a planned two-footed landing, so there is no
  uncontrolled fall to catch;
- the run is short and along the longer side, with a stated clear zone;
- the floor and footwear conditions are stated, with an explicit way out;
- it keeps a falling-start pattern, so the transfer to the outdoor drill is real.

## Assumptions that need a coach's yes

1. 2 yards and 10 ft clear are conservative numbers I chose, not source numbers.
2. Four reps is acceptable for a lower-speed version; fewer may be better for
   younger players (Foundation already uses 2-3).
3. Whether to show this at all at Foundation level indoors, or to default to the
   wall-drive fallback.
